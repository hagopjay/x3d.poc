## The lineage in one paragraph

One legend (Abgar, the Image of Edessa, images not made by hands) is the trunk. Three branches grow from it: the Byzantine line (the Pantocrator and the Spas Nerukotvorny icons), the Shroud line (the Shroud's own history and the painted contact-relic copies pressed against it), and the Salvator Mundi line, which descends from the Pantocrator without needing any single relic. The West's Veronica legend runs in parallel. Which way influence ran between the Shroud and the icon tradition is contested; both readings are recorded as claims.

## Claims by epistemic tag

### Measured

- **Face and hands appear faintly on the reverse side** (supported by The double superficiality of the frontal image of the Turin Shroud)
- **The 1988 fragments are consistent with the main cloth** (by Freer-Waters and Jull 2026)
- **The blood was on the cloth before the image** (supported by A chemical investigation of the Shroud of Turin; supported by Kearse 2026)
- **The body image is oxidized, dehydrated cellulose, not pigment** (by STURP (Shroud of Turin Research Project); supported by STURP summary of findings; supported by Fanti et al. 2010)
- **The cloth dates to 1260 to 1390**. Three laboratories, 1988. The sampling and homogeneity are disputed; alternatives are considered fringe by mainstream science. (supported by Radiocarbon dating of the Shroud of Turin; disputed by Riani et al. 2013; disputed by Casabianca et al. 2019; disputed by De Caro et al. 2022)
- **The image is not discernible closer than about 1.8 m** (supported by Correlation of image intensity on the Turin Shroud with the 3-D structure of a human body shape)
- **The weave can be reproduced only on a treadle loom** (by Antoinette Merete Olsen)

### Inferred

- **Image intensity encodes the vertical cloth-body distance** (supported by Correlation of image intensity on the Turin Shroud with the 3-D structure of a human body shape; examined by Ercoline et al. 1982)

### Historical

- **A bishop wrote in 1389 that the cloth was painted by a confessed artist** (by Pierre d'Arcis, Bishop of Troyes)

### Hypothesis

- **Trace iron oxide on the cloth came from painted copies touched to it**

### Tradition

- **The 1624 copy developed a damp mark at the wound site when touched to the original**. The monastery's own tradition.

### Contested

- **145 points of correspondence between the Justinian II solidus and the Shroud face** (by Alan and Judith Whanger)
- **170 points of congruence between the Sinai Pantocrator and the Shroud face** (by Alan and Judith Whanger)
- **Byzantine icons carry the Shroud's facial traits**. Not accepted as settled by mainstream art historians. (by Paul Vignon)
- **Dürer's Salvator Mundi corresponds closely to the Shroud face**. Made by some Shroud researchers; not peer-reviewed art-historical consensus.
- **Pollen, plant images and coins identify the cloth's history** (by Frei 1973; by Filas 1982; by Danin 1999; by Barcaccia et al. 2015)
- **The Salvator Mundi is by Leonardo's own hand**. Some scholars think it studio work or a later copy.
- **The Shroud and the Image of Edessa are the same cloth**. A published hypothesis, not a scholarly consensus. (by Ian Wilson)
- **The Shroud is a 13th to 14th century work that borrowed the Byzantine Christ face**
- **The Shroud, as the Image of Edessa, shaped icon painting from the 6th century**
- **The Sudarium's stains are consistent with the Shroud's face**. Photographic overlay and forensic comparison; the honest verb is 'consistent with'. (supported by Whanger et al. 1985; supported by Heras 1998)
- **The image is red ochre and vermilion in a collagen binder** (by Walter McCrone; by Judgment Day for the Shroud of Turin; disputed by STURP summary of findings; disputed by A chemical investigation of the Shroud of Turin)
- **The image's distortions match cloth draped over a low-relief sculpture** (by Cicero Moraes; by Moraes 2025; disputed by Casabianca et al. 2026; answered by Moraes 2026)
- **The radiocarbon raw data trend along the sample** (by Riani et al. 2013; by Casabianca et al. 2019)

### Unsupported

- **Andy Warhol made a work titled after the Shroud**. Could not be verified; his 1986 Last Supper series is the documented link to this tradition.
- **Salvador Dalí painted a Salvator Mundi**. No such painting is known; 'Salvador' and 'Salvator' are the same word.
